\section*{0. The model and the central objects}
\label{sec:defs}

All experiments use one estimator, the \emph{layered mixture} of the
companion paper.  This section defines every object that the tasks
below compute with; nothing later is used before it is defined here.

\field{The prior, level by level}
Fix an alphabet of size $d$.  For a \emph{level} (depth) $L \ge 1$,
attach to each symbol $x$ the weight
\[
  W_x \;=\; \prod_{\ell=1}^{L} E_{x,\ell},
  \qquad E_{x,\ell} \sim \Exp(1) \text{ i.i.d.},
\]
and let the random probability vector be $p_x = W_x / \sum_{y=1}^{d}
W_y$.  Larger $L$ produces sparser, rougher vectors; $L$ plays the
role of a complexity (depth) parameter.  The full estimator averages
uniformly over $L = 1, \dots, L_{\max}$ with $L_{\max} =
\lceil 2 c^{*} \ln d \rceil$ ($c^{*} \approx 2.365$, the constant of
the companion paper); measured $L_{\max} = 33$--$59$.

\field{Profiles}
A \emph{row} is a vector of successor counts $(m_x)_{x=1}^{d}$,
$m_x \ge 0$ (how often each next-symbol followed a given context).
Its \emph{profile} $\prof = (m_1, \dots, m_s)$ is the multiset of the
$s$ \emph{nonzero} counts, sorted; $N = \sum_i m_i$ is the row total.
By exchangeability of the prior, all quantities below depend on a row
only through its profile --- the basis of all deduplication.  We
write $\tilde k \le s$ for the number of \emph{distinct} count values
in $\prof$, and $\mu_c$ for the multiplicity of the value $c$.

\field{The profile probability}
The central scalar is the probability that the level-$L$ model
assigns to (any one) sequence with profile $\prof$:
\begin{equation}
  q_L(\prof) \;=\;
  \mathbb{E}\Bigl[\, \prod_{x : m_x > 0} p_x^{\,m_x} \Bigr],
  \qquad
  q_{\mathrm{avg}}(\prof) \;=\; \frac{1}{L_{\max}}
  \sum_{L=1}^{L_{\max}} q_L(\prof).
  \label{eq:qdef}
\end{equation}
Every codelength in the project is a sum of $-\log_2
q_{\mathrm{avg}}$ terms over rows; every predictive probability is a
ratio of two $q_{\mathrm{avg}}$ values.

\field{The integral representation}
The normalization $\sum_y W_y$ is removed by the Gamma identity
$(\sum_y W_y)^{-N} = \frac{1}{\Gamma(N)} \int_0^\infty t^{N-1}
e^{-t \sum_y W_y}\, dt$.  Because the $W_x$ are i.i.d., the
expectation factorizes under the integral, giving the exact formula
\begin{equation}
  q_L(\prof) \;=\; \frac{1}{\Gamma(N)} \int_0^{\infty} t^{N-1}
  \prod_{x=1}^{d} \varphi^{(L)}_{m_x}(t)\, dt,
  \qquad
  \varphi^{(L)}_{r}(t) \;:=\; \mathbb{E}\bigl[\,Y^r e^{-tY}\bigr],
  \;\; Y = \prod_{\ell=1}^{L} E_\ell .
  \label{eq:integral}
\end{equation}
Here $\varphi^{(L)}_r$ --- the \emph{moment function} --- is the
$r$-th moment of a product of $L$ independent unit exponentials,
damped by $e^{-tY}$; $t > 0$ is a \emph{scalar} integration variable.
The $d - s$ symbols with $m_x = 0$ contribute the common factor
$\varphi^{(L)}_0(t)^{\,d-s}$.  Numerically everything lives on the
logarithmic axis $u = \ln t$ (a scalar), on a finite grid
$u_1 < \dots < u_G$ spanning $[u_{\min}, u_{\max}]$ (measured $G =
3.6\cdot 10^4$ at the largest settings), and in log space:
with the \emph{log-integrand}
\begin{equation}
  \psi_L(u) \;=\; N u \;+\; (d - s) \ln \varphi^{(L)}_0(e^u)
  \;+\; \sum_{\text{distinct } c \in \prof} \mu_c
  \ln \varphi^{(L)}_c(e^u),
  \label{eq:psi}
\end{equation}
one has $q_L(\prof) = \frac{1}{\Gamma(N)} \int e^{\psi_L(u)}\, du$
(the substitution $t = e^u$ contributes the extra factor $t$ that
turns $t^{N-1}$ into $e^{Nu}$).

\field{Two exact boundary facts}
(i) $\varphi^{(1)}_r(t) = \Gamma(r+1)\,(1+t)^{-(r+1)}$ (closed form,
the recursion's base).  (ii) As $t \to 0$,
$\varphi^{(L)}_r(t) \to \Gamma(r+1)^{L}$; hence to the left of the
grid $\psi_L(u) = Nu + \mathrm{const} + O(e^u)$ and the left tail of
the integral is available in closed form.  For $L = 1$ the whole
integral is closed-form (a Dirichlet--multinomial); no tables are
needed.

\section{T1 --- Building the moment tables}

\field{Task}
Tabulate the moment functions $\varphi^{(L)}_r$ of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:integral} on the $u$-grid, for every level $L \le
L_{\max}$ and every count value $r$ that occurs in the data.  Built
once per alphabet size, cached on disk, read by all later tasks.

\field{Formula}
Conditioning on the last factor $E_L = e$ of $Y = Y' E_L$ gives the
recursion in the level:
\begin{equation}
  \varphi^{(\ell)}_r(t)
  \;=\; \mathbb{E}\bigl[E^r\, \varphi^{(\ell-1)}_r(tE)\bigr]
  \;=\; \int_0^\infty e^{-e}\, e^{r}\,
  \varphi^{(\ell-1)}_r(t e)\, de,
  \qquad
  \varphi^{(1)}_r(t) = \frac{\Gamma(r+1)}{(1+t)^{r+1}} .
  \label{eq:recursion}
\end{equation}

\field{Input/Output}
In: the set $\mathcal{R}$ of needed count values (from the data:
every value occurring in any profile), $L_{\max}$, the grid
$\{u_g\}_{g=1}^{G}$.  Out: the array $\ln \varphi^{(L)}_r(e^{u_g})
\in \mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{R}| \times L_{\max} \times G}$, stored on
disk (one file per $r$).

\field{Algorithm}
For each $r$ independently: start from the closed form at $\ell = 1$;
for $\ell = 2, \dots, L_{\max}$ evaluate
Eq.~\eqref{eq:recursion} at every grid point by Gauss--Laguerre
quadrature of order $Q$, reading $\varphi^{(\ell-1)}_r$ at the
shifted points $u_g + \ln e_j$ by linear interpolation in $u$ (with
the exact left fill $\Gamma(r+1)^{\ell-1}$ beyond the grid).
Embarrassingly parallel over $r$; new $r$ values are built
incrementally as the data produces them.

\field{Cost and scaling}
Build: $O(|\mathcal{R}| \cdot L_{\max} \cdot G \cdot Q)$ operations
($\approx 10^{12}$ at the largest settings; measured $45$ minutes on
$64$ cores).  Storage: $8\,|\mathcal{R}| L_{\max} G$ bytes $=
85$\,GB there.  $|\mathcal{R}|$ grows slowly with corpus size (new
count values become rare); $G$ must grow like the \emph{span} of the
$u$-axis, i.e.\ with $\ln(\max r)$ and $L_{\max}$; $L_{\max} \propto
\ln d$.  The recurring cost is \emph{reading}: T2/T3 gather $\tilde
k$ table rows per profile per level, so layout and locality dominate
in practice (network filesystems: measured failure mode).

\field{Speedup routes}
(1)~[exact] One shared cache across all experiments (measured waste:
$219$\,GB of duplicate tables).
(2)~[exact] Level-major single-file layout $+$ float32: $4\times$
smaller, one file open per level instead of $|\mathcal{R}|$.
(3)~[approx] Large-$r$ asymptotics: for $r \gg 1$,
$\varphi^{(L)}_r$ concentrates (the integrand of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:recursion} peaks sharply) and approaches a scaling
form in $(r, t r^{L})$; replacing tables beyond a threshold
$r^{\ast}$ by the asymptotic formula shrinks $\mathcal{R}$ to the
small counts where tables are genuinely needed.  Needs a worked-out
error bound before use.

\section{T2 --- Evaluating a profile probability (the atom)}

\field{Task}
Compute $\log q_L(\prof)$ for all levels $L$, hence $\log
q_{\mathrm{avg}}(\prof)$, for one profile; executed once per unique
profile per model (static runs) and once per refresh per active row
(sequential runs).

\field{Formula}
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:qdef}--\eqref{eq:psi}: $\log q_L =
\log \int e^{\psi_L(u)} du - \ln \Gamma(N)$.

\field{Input/Output}
In: profile $\prof$ as pairs (distinct value $c$, multiplicity
$\mu_c$); the tables (T1).  Out: the vector $\bigl(\log_2
q_L(\prof)\bigr)_{L=1}^{L_{\max}}$ and $\log_2 q_{\mathrm{avg}}$.

\field{Algorithm}
Per level: (i) assemble $\psi_L$ on the grid by
Eq.~\eqref{eq:psi} --- $\tilde k + 1$ table rows, $O(G \tilde k)$;
(ii) locate every local maximum of $\psi_L$ within a significance
window of the global one (the integrand can be multimodal: deep
levels with heavy counts produce a second, far-left peak);
(iii) refine each peak by a bracketed saddle solve (the derivative
$\psi_L'(u)$ is available from neighboring table rows) and add
Laplace contributions by log-sum-exp; (iv) add the closed-form left
tail of Section~0.  Special cases short-circuit: $N \le 1$ by
symmetry, $L = 1$ closed form.  Batched over profiles; parallelized
over profile chunks, each level's table rows distributed to workers
through shared memory.

\field{Cost and scaling}
Dominated by step (i): $O(L_{\max}\, G\, \tilde k)$ per profile ---
from $4\cdot 10^6$ ops ($\tilde k = 2$) to $2\cdot 10^8$ ($\tilde k
\sim 100$).  Measured: $7\cdot 10^4$ unique profiles at $L_{\max} =
59$ in $17$ minutes on $64$ cores.  Static models invoke T2 only $U$
times (deduplication); sequential models invoke it per refresh (T8).

\field{Speedup routes}
(1)~[exact] \emph{Warm-started peaks / local grids}: the full-grid
sweep exists only to locate peaks robustly.  Peak locations vary
smoothly with the profile (and, across refreshes of the same row,
barely move); warm-starting the saddle solve from a stored or
predicted location and evaluating $\psi_L$ only on a local window
around it replaces $G$ by $G_{\mathrm{loc}} \sim 10^2$: up to
$300\times$ on step (i), with a cheap global-sweep fallback when the
bracketed solve fails or the result looks suspicious.
(2)~[approx] \emph{Level truncation with certified bounds}: the
$L$-sum of Eq.~\eqref{eq:qdef} is dominated by a few levels around
the maximizing depth, with geometric decay away from it; exact
evaluation of the top levels plus a tail bound replaces $L_{\max}$
by $L_{\mathrm{eff}} \sim 5$--$10$.
(3)~[approx] Coarser grids for small-$N$ profiles (their integrands
are wide and smooth); per-profile grid selection by target accuracy.

\section{T3 --- The predictive table of a row}

\field{Task}
Turn one row's counts into a next-symbol probability distribution ---
the workhorse of sequential (pooled) codes, executed at every refresh
for every row of every expert.

\field{Formula}
For a symbol whose current count in the row is $c$ (including $c = 0$
for the unseen symbols, when $s < d$):
\begin{equation}
  p(\text{symbol} \mid \text{row}) \;=\;
  \frac{q_{\mathrm{avg}}(\prof \oplus c)}{q_{\mathrm{avg}}(\prof)},
  \qquad
  \prof \oplus c := \prof \text{ with one count } c \to c + 1
  \label{eq:pred}
\end{equation}
(exchangeability again: the value depends on the symbol only through
$c$, so the table has $\tilde k + 1$ distinct entries; the entries
sum to one exactly, and are renormalized to absorb quadrature error,
measured $\sim 5\cdot 10^{-5}$ relative).

\field{Input/Output}
In: the row's counts; tables (T1).  Out: $\log_2 p$ over the
alphabet ($\tilde k + 1$ distinct values).

\field{Algorithm}
Today: one T2 evaluation for $\prof$ and one for each of the $\tilde
k + 1$ augmented profiles $\prof \oplus c$ --- i.e.\ $\tilde k + 2$
independent atom evaluations per row, memoized by profile.

\field{Cost and scaling}
$O(L_{\max}\, G\, \tilde k^{2})$ per row.  Measured workload:
$5.9\cdot 10^6$ atom evaluations for the $V = 1{,}024$, $C = 32$,
six-lag pooled run --- the dominant cost of sequential codes, and
memoization is weak because active rows change between refreshes.

\field{Speedup routes}
(1)~[exact; the largest single lever] \emph{Shared integrand}: by
Eq.~\eqref{eq:psi}, every augmented profile differs from the base in
ONE factor:
\[
  q_L(\prof \oplus c) \;=\; \frac{1}{\Gamma(N{+}1)} \int
  e^{\psi_L(u)}\; e^{u}\,
  \frac{\varphi^{(L)}_{c+1}(e^u)}{\varphi^{(L)}_{c}(e^u)}\; du .
\]
Assemble $\psi_L$ once ($O(G \tilde k)$); each predictive entry is
then a single $O(G)$ weighted sum against a ratio of two table rows.
Per-row cost falls from $O(G \tilde k^{2})$ to $O(G \tilde k)$: a
factor $\tilde k$ ($\sim 100\times$ exactly on the rich rows that
dominate), with bit-identical output up to quadrature details.
(2)~[exact] \emph{Incremental integrand across refreshes}: between
refreshes few counts of a row change; update $\psi_L' = \psi_L +
\sum_{\text{changed}} \bigl(\ln \varphi^{(L)}_{c'} - \ln
\varphi^{(L)}_{c}\bigr)$ in $O(G \cdot \#\text{changed})$ instead of
reassembling.  Requires storing $\psi_L$ for active rows
($G \times L_{\max}$ float32 $\approx 8$\,MB per row at the largest
settings --- affordable exactly for the expensive frequent rows).
(3)~T2's routes apply on top (warm peaks, level truncation).

\section{T4 --- Counting: rows, profiles, deduplication}

\field{Task}
Extract from the token stream, for a given state map, the
successor-count rows, their profiles, and the row-to-unique-profile
map.

\field{Formula}
$\mathrm{count}[s, x] = \#\{t : \sigma(x^{t-1}) = s,\ x_t = x\}$ for
a state map $\sigma$ (last token, product state, tree context, or
lag-$\delta$ token); $\mathrm{profile}(s)$ as in Section~0; the
dedup map $s \mapsto$ profile id.

\field{Input/Output}
In: token ids ($n$ integers), $\sigma$.  Out: $\mathcal{N}$ rows
($P$ nonzero entries), $U$ unique profiles, dedup map.

\field{Algorithm}
One pass with hash-map counting; per-row sort of nonzero counts;
profile hashing.

\field{Cost and scaling}
Time $O(n)$ per state map --- minutes, never the bottleneck.
\emph{Memory} $O(P)$ with a dictionary constant of $30$--$60$ bytes
per entry: measured $\approx 200$\,GB at $P \approx 1.5\cdot 10^7$
(full vocabulary, depth $2$) --- this is what forbids depth $3$
today.  Deduplication is the great equalizer downstream: $P =
4.4\cdot 10^6$ contexts collapse to $U = 7\cdot 10^4$ profiles.

\field{Speedup routes}
(1)~[exact] External sort-and-merge counting: $O(n \log n)$ on disk,
constant memory; standard technology.
(2)~[exact] Suffix-array counting: one compact $O(n)$-memory index of
the token stream yields the counts of \emph{all} contexts of
\emph{all} depths (each context is an interval of the suffix array);
the natural route to depth $\ge 3$ at full vocabulary.
(3)~[exact] Fixed-width integer arrays instead of dictionary objects.

\section{T5 --- Aggregation: families, trees, posteriors}

\field{Task}
Combine per-row atoms into model codelengths, mixtures over model
families, and posteriors.

\field{Formula}
Member codelength $\sum_{\text{rows } s} -\log_2
q_{\mathrm{avg}}(\prof_s)$; the context-tree mixture over all
prunings of the depth-$D$ context trie by the recursion
\[
  \beta(v) = \tfrac12\, q_{\mathrm{avg}}(\prof_v) + \tfrac12
  \prod_{c \in \mathrm{children}(v)} \beta(c)
\]
(bottom-up over observed contexts $v$); a family mixture over $K$
members $= \mathrm{logsumexp}$ of member log-likelihood totals minus
$\log_2 K$; the posterior is the normalized exponential of the same
totals.

\field{Input/Output/Algorithm/Cost}
In: per-profile $\log_2 q_{\mathrm{avg}}$ (T2), trie structure.
Out: bits/token per member, family bits, posterior.  Single passes,
$O(\mathcal{N})$, $O(P)$, $O(K)$; measured seconds.  No scaling
concern.

\section{T6 --- The additive pooling pass}

\field{Task}
Sequentially code the corpus under every additive (mixture) weight
vector of the grid simultaneously.

\field{Formula}
With experts $e = 0$ (memoryless) and $e = 1..D$ (lag $\delta_e$),
per-step probabilities $p_e(x_t)$ read from the T3 tables of the
current refresh window, and weight vectors $\lambda \in \Lambda$,
$|\Lambda| = K$:
\[
  \mathrm{bits}(\lambda) = \sum_{t} -\log_2 \sum_{e=0}^{D}
  \lambda_e\, p_e(x_t) .
\]

\field{Input/Output}
In: the $n \times (D{+}1)$ matrix of realized-token probabilities
(one gather per expert per step); $\Lambda$.  Out: $K$ codelengths.

\field{Algorithm/Cost}
Blocked gather, one $(\text{block} \times (D{+}1)) \cdot
((D{+}1) \times K)$ matrix product per block, log, accumulate:
$O(n (D{+}1) K) \approx 10^{10}$ flops --- minutes.  Linear in
everything; never a concern.

\section{T7 --- The multiplicative pooling pass}

\field{Task}
Same as T6 for the tempered-product rule, which must renormalize over
the alphabet at \emph{every} position.

\field{Formula}
With exponents $\beta \in B$, $|B| = K_{\mathrm{prod}}$:
\[
  p_\beta(x_t) = \frac{s_\beta(x_t)}{Z_t}, \qquad
  s_\beta(x) = q_0(x) \prod_{j=1}^{D}
  \Bigl(\frac{p_j(x)}{q_0(x)}\Bigr)^{\beta_j}, \qquad
  Z_t = \sum_{x=1}^{V} s_\beta(x),
\]
where $q_0$ is the memoryless expert's T3 table.

\field{Input/Output}
In: T3 tables of the $D$ experts and $q_0$; $B$.  Out:
$K_{\mathrm{prod}}$ codelengths.

\field{Algorithm (today) and cost}
Dense: per block, gather each expert's full table rows for the
occurring contexts, form the $V$-vector of logits per step per
member, log-sum-exp.  $O(n\, V\, D\, K_{\mathrm{prod}})$: measured
$68$ minutes at $V = 4{,}096$; $\sim 10^{16}$ at $V = 10^6$, $n =
1.5\cdot 10^8$.  \textbf{The only truly bad exponent in the
project.}

\field{Speedup route}
[exact; kills the $V$ factor]  By Eq.~\eqref{eq:pred} every T3 table
is ``one background value plus $s_j$ exceptions'': $p_j(x) = a_{j}$
for every $x$ unseen in row $j$'s counts, individual values only on
the $s_j$ observed successors.  Let $S_t = \bigcup_j
\mathrm{supp}_j(t)$ (union of the exception sets; $|S_t| \sim
10^2$--$10^3 \ll V$).  For $x \notin S_t$ the product collapses to a
constant times $q_0(x)^{1 - \sum_j \beta_j}$, so
\[
  Z_t = \Bigl(\prod_j a_{j,t}^{\beta_j}\Bigr)
  \Bigl( T_\gamma - \!\!\sum_{x \in S_t}\! q_0(x)^{\gamma} \Bigr)
  + \sum_{x \in S_t} s_\beta(x),
  \qquad
  \gamma = 1 - \textstyle\sum_j \beta_j,
  \quad T_\gamma = \textstyle\sum_{x=1}^{V} q_0(x)^{\gamma},
\]
with the power sums $T_\gamma$ precomputed once per refresh per
needed $\gamma$.  Exact $Z_t$ in $O(|S_t| D)$ per step per member
instead of $O(V D)$.

\section{T8 --- Refresh scheduling of sequential codes}

\field{Task}
Decide when each expert row's predictive table (T3) is rebuilt.  Any
schedule that depends only on the past yields a valid sequential
code; staleness only lengthens it.  This schedule is the multiplier
on T3 that separates static runs (minutes) from pooled runs (hours).

\field{Formula}
Current schedule: a global clock; all rows rebuilt at $C$ equally
spaced positions; T3 invocations $= C \times (\text{active rows})$.

\field{Input/Output}
In: running counts.  Out: refresh times per row; the tables used
between refreshes.

\field{Cost and scaling}
T3 cost $\times C$, with memoization weakened by row churn (measured:
$5.9\cdot 10^6$ atom evaluations at $V = 1{,}024$, $C = 32$).

\field{Speedup routes}
(1)~[valid code; controlled staleness] \emph{Growth-triggered
refreshes}: rebuild a row when its total $N$ has grown by a factor
$1 + \epsilon$ since its last rebuild.  Refreshes per row drop from
$C$ to $O(\log_{1+\epsilon} N_{\mathrm{final}})$, staleness is
bounded \emph{relatively} (the quantity that matters for predictive
quality), and the trigger is past-measurable, so the code remains
valid.  Combined with T3's shared/incremental integrands the
compound gain on pooled runs is estimated at $10^{2}$--$10^{3}$.
(2)~[exact] Shard rows across cluster nodes (rows are independent
given the schedule).

\section*{Scaling summary and discussion priority}

At $10\times$ the corpus (enwik9-scale: $n \approx 1.4\cdot 10^8$,
$V \sim 10^6$) with today's algorithms: T4 needs terabytes of RAM
(blocked); T3$\times$T8 explodes (richer rows, $C$ multiplier:
weeks); T7 dense is impossible; T1/T2 grow only through
$|\mathcal{R}|$, $G$, $L_{\max}$ (manageable).  With the [exact]
routes --- shared and incremental integrands (T3), warm-started
peaks (T2), sparse normalization (T7), growth-triggered refreshes
(T8), external or suffix-array counting (T4), sharding --- every
task returns to node-hours; the [approx] routes (level truncation,
large-$r$ asymptotics, coarse grids) are held in reserve, each with
a checkable error.

Suggested discussion order: T3(1) shared integrand; T7 sparse
normalizer; T8 growth-triggered refreshes; T2(1) warm-started peaks;
T4 suffix arrays; then the approximations if still needed.
