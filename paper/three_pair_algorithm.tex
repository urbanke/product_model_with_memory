\section{Purpose and statistical object}

Let $Y=X_t$, $A=X_{t-1}$, and $B=X_{t-2}$ after mapping the retained
tokenizer IDs to an alphabet $\cA$ of size $V$ (including escape).  At each
causal checkpoint the layered estimator supplies three pairwise target
margins
\[
 p_{YA}(y,a),\qquad p_{YB}(y,b),\qquad p_{AB}(a,b).
\]
The central estimator is the maximum-entropy distribution having these three
margins.  We parameterize its conditional distribution as
\begin{equation}
 q(y\mid a,b)
 =\frac{p_0(y)\exp\{c_1(y,a)+c_2(y,b)\}}{Z_{ab}},
 \qquad
 Z_{ab}=\sum_y p_0(y)\exp\{c_1(y,a)+c_2(y,b)\}.
 \label{eq:model}
\end{equation}
Here $p_0$ is the chosen unigram baseline.  The factors $c_1,c_2$ are fitted
so that $p_{AB}(a,b)q(y\mid a,b)$ has the prescribed $YA$ and $YB$
margins.  The $AB$ margin is used directly as the context distribution and
is therefore not discarded.

The implementation never constructs a dense $V^3$ joint distribution or a
dense $V\times V$ table.  It stores observed pair supports and traverses only
compatible sparse intersections.

\section{Stage I: causal checkpoint construction}

The token stream is divided into 32 geometrically increasing prefixes.  For
each prefix, cumulative lag-one and lag-two counts are converted by the
layered universal estimator into sparse pair probabilities.  The two
margins involving $Y$ are projected to a common $Y$ marginal, using a sparse
Sinkhorn/IPF projection to a tolerance much tighter than the subsequent
calibration tolerance.  The result saved for checkpoint $k$ contains
\begin{itemize}
\item the active $AB$, $YA$, and $YB$ pair supports and probabilities;
\item the common target marginal of $Y$;
\item the baseline $\log p_0(y)$;
\item sparse fallback corrections used outside the active supports.
\end{itemize}
Checkpoint construction is currently the dominant elapsed-time stage.  The
stream is processed cumulatively and completed checkpoints are restartable,
but much of the layered-estimator work is still repeated for different
prefixes.  Parallel profile evaluation uses worker processes and therefore
has a significant worker-private memory component.

For planning, the present hypothesis is that construction time is initially
approximately linear in stream length $N$ and retained alphabet size $V$,
with saturation as new tokens cease to create new sparse supports.  This is
an empirical hypothesis, not an algorithmic theorem.

\section{Stable global supports and birth depths}

After all checkpoints exist, every final pair support is sorted into a stable
birth-major order.  Each edge receives the first checkpoint at which it is
active.  Consequently, checkpoint $k$ is represented by prefixes of the
global $YA$, $YB$, and $AB$ arrays, and one shared intersection topology can
serve the entire checkpoint chain.

Write
\[
 \E_{YA}=\{(y,a)\},\quad
 \E_{YB}=\{(y,b)\},\quad
 \E_{AB}=\{(a,b)\}.
\]
The compatible intersection set is
\begin{equation}
 \T=\{(y,a,b):(y,a)\in\E_{YA},\ (y,b)\in\E_{YB},\
                    (a,b)\in\E_{AB}\}.
 \label{eq:triangles}
\end{equation}
Every triangle also stores the maximum of the birth depths of its three pair
edges.  At checkpoint $k$ it is traversed exactly when that depth is at most
$k$.  Let $T=|\T|$ and let $E_{YA},E_{YB},E_{AB}$ denote the pair-edge
counts.

\section{Sparse evaluation of the exponential family}

Set
\[
 r_1(y,a)=e^{c_1(y,a)}-1,
 \qquad
 r_2(y,b)=e^{c_2(y,b)}-1,
\]
and
\[
 S_{1a}=\sum_y p_0(y)r_1(y,a),
 \qquad
 S_{2b}=\sum_y p_0(y)r_2(y,b).
\]
Expanding the product in \eqref{eq:model} gives
\begin{equation}
 Z_{ab}=1+S_{1a}+S_{2b}
 +\sum_{y:(y,a,b)\in\T}p_0(y)r_1(y,a)r_2(y,b).
 \label{eq:sparseZ}
\end{equation}
Thus the only joint correction is a traversal of the compatible triangles;
all absent combinations are handled by the first three terms.  A reverse
traversal accumulates the resulting $Y$, $YA$, and $YB$ model margins.
Numerically dangerous signed cancellations are detected and recomputed by a
positive log-sum-exp calculation.

One complete exact margin evaluation costs
\[
 O(V+E_{YA}+E_{YB}+E_{AB}+T)
\]
time and corresponding pair/topology storage.  In a fully dense mathematical
limit $E=O(V^2)$ and $T=O(V^3)$, but the observed corpus supports are far
sparser and saturate strongly at fixed $N$.

\section{The two current traversal layouts}

The implementation presently persists two memory-mapped orderings of the
same triangle set.

\paragraph{YA-major layout.}
Rows are global $YA$ edges.  Each triangle stores its $YB$ and $AB$ indices
and birth depth; $y$ is implicit in the row.  This layout permits an efficient
parallel reverse pass with disjoint ownership of $YA$ rows.  It is used for
exact certificates and exact fallback.

\paragraph{AB-major layout.}
Rows are global $AB$ edges.  Each triangle stores its $YA$ and $YB$ indices
and birth depth; $(a,b)$ is implicit in the row.  A contiguous range of rows
is exactly one stochastic edge block, so sampled gradients traverse the
memory-mapped graph directly and build no checkpoint-local topology.

The layouts are not mathematically required.  They are the current fastest
engineering compromise.  At V1024 each occupies about 0.704 GiB.  The
AB-major exact evaluator is currently about 1.9 times slower than YA-major at
the final checkpoint, so both are retained.  A likely final design stores
triangle fields once and adds two compact traversal indices, or improves the
AB-major exact kernel enough to remove the YA-major copy.

\section{Stage II: calibration}

Checkpoints are fitted sequentially.  The fitted factors from checkpoint
$k-1$ are copied into the common coordinates of checkpoint $k$; new
coordinates start from zero.  This warm start becomes increasingly effective
because adjacent late checkpoints have similar target distributions.

The main solver is a parallel stochastic dual method.  The active $AB$ edges
are divided into 128 approximately triangle-balanced blocks.  Each update
samples a fixed total batch of 12 blocks/replicas; changing the number of
workers does not change this statistical batch.  Workers evaluate sampled
AB-major ranges in native C and their gradients are combined before one
global update.  The learning rate follows the implemented plateau scheduler.

Every 50 updates, the complete YA-major graph computes the exact grouped
margin certificate
\[
 \max\{\|\widehat p_Y-p_Y\|_1,
        \|\widehat p_{YA}-p_{YA}\|_1,
        \|\widehat p_{YB}-p_{YB}\|_1\}.
\]
The checkpoint is accepted when this is below $0.01$.  If the stochastic
budget ends first, the exact layered L-BFGS solver is invoked as a fallback at
that checkpoint.  Fallback is a correctness mechanism, not evidence that the
graph representation failed.  The current V1024 production run imposed a
500-update stochastic cap and therefore used fallback at 10 early or
intermediate checkpoints; fallback itself consumed only 2.28 seconds.

Calibration does not scan the corpus.  Its dependence on $N$ is only through
the support sizes and $T$, which eventually saturate.  Its approximate cost is
\[
 I_s\,T_s + I_e\,T,
\]
where $I_s$ is the number of stochastic updates, $T_s$ the triangles in a
sampled batch, and $I_e$ the number of full certificates and fallback margin
passes.  The dominant measured component is stochastic evaluation, not exact
fallback.

\section{Stage III: causal scoring and honest accounting}

Checkpoint $k$ predicts the following interval using only factors and
probabilities constructed from its prefix.  The 31 independent intervals are
scheduled longest-first across worker processes, while their contributions
are accumulated in causal order.  Exact positive log-sum-exp evaluation is
used.  Scoring time is approximately linear in the number of scored tokens;
with memory mapping or streaming, its working memory need not be linear in
the file length.

The reported honest code in bits per original byte is the sum of
\begin{enumerate}
\item reduced-alphabet predictive length, including the initial prefix;
\item a one-time description of the retained tokenizer vocabulary;
\item a sequential payload code for original tokenizer IDs behind escape.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Current measurements}

For text8, the final support sizes at fixed stream length are:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{rrrr}
\toprule
$V$ & $E_{AB}=E_{YA}$ & $E_{YB}$ & $T$ \\
\midrule
1024  & 338,237   & 537,579   & 83,702,774 \\
4096  & 1,399,764 & 2,288,630 & 279,788,925 \\
8192  & 2,269,603 & 3,664,436 & 368,845,867 \\
16384 & 3,166,823 & 5,027,113 & 418,852,400 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Thus a sixteen-fold increase in $V$ produced only a five-fold increase in
$T$ at fixed text8 length.  From V8192 to V16384, doubling $V$ increased $T$
by only 14 percent.  The V16384 point uses one final checkpoint; pair and
triangle counts are directly comparable, while its one-layer byte count is
not directly comparable with the 32-layer stores.

At fixed $V=16384$, controlled two-worker final-prefix constructions give:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{rrrrrr}
\toprule
$N$ & construction & reported peak & $E_{AB}$ & $E_{YB}$ & $T$ \\
\midrule
4,857,324  & 624.9 s  & 3.67 GB & 1,222,978 & 1,795,605 & 65,600,208 \\
9,714,647  & 962.3 s  & 4.13 GB & 2,005,070 & 3,063,157 & 170,164,657 \\
19,429,294 & 1507.9 s & 7.17 GB & 3,166,823 & 5,027,113 & 418,852,400 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Construction time grows only by factors 1.54 and 1.57 when $N$ doubles in
this range, because fixed work and support saturation offset the longer
stream.  Pair supports grow sublinearly.  Compatible triples nevertheless
grow by factors 2.59 and 2.46, approximately $N^{1.3}$ over these three
points: additional pair edges create many new compatible intersections.
Consequently, saturation of the one-token vocabulary does not by itself
imply that pair intersections have saturated.  This measured local exponent
is an interpolation, not an asymptotic law; with fixed finite $V$, all pair
and triangle supports must eventually saturate.
On macOS the reported resident statistic is a per-process/resource-usage
peak, not the simultaneous sum of the parent and worker RSS values.  It is
comparable across these identically configured runs but must not be used as
the whole-job memory requirement.  Spot checks showed that aggregate
concurrent RSS can be larger; future benchmark drivers should sample and sum
the complete process tree.

The current full V1024/C32 shared-graph calibration took 108.7 seconds with
12 workers and peaked at 3.71 GiB RSS, versus 142.5 seconds and about 9.55 GiB
for the preceding compact-plan implementation.  Scoring took 52.3 seconds.
Upstream construction of its 32 checkpoint states took about 871 seconds.
For the diagnostic V16384 final prefix, upstream construction with two
workers took 1507.9 seconds and reported a 7.17-GB resource peak, whereas constructing the
one-layer shared intersection graph from that state took 21.4 seconds.

\section{Where improvement is most likely}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Remove repeated checkpoint-construction work.}  Construction is
currently the largest demonstrated elapsed-time stage.  Counts, profile
families, projections, and support births should be reused more aggressively
across prefixes.
\item \textbf{Store triangles once.}  Replace the two complete traversal
layouts by one canonical triangle payload plus compact YA- and AB-order
indices, after measuring the memory/time tradeoff at larger $V$.
\item \textbf{Reduce stochastic passes.}  Certificate-driven scheduling,
stronger warm transfer, or a standard second-order finish is valuable only if
it removes a substantial number of sampled margin evaluations.
\item \textbf{Control worker replication.}  Construction memory has a shared
component plus a worker-private component; worker count must be selected from
measured elapsed-time/RSS curves rather than maximized blindly.
\item \textbf{Preserve streaming.}  Increasing enwik8 to enwik9 should make
construction and scoring time roughly linear in file length, but should not
multiply their working memory.  Calibration grows only as new supports and
triangles appear and should eventually saturate in $N$.
\end{enumerate}
